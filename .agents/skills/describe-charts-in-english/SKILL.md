# Describe charts in English

## Read the figure

- Read the task and inspect the title, axes, scale, units, legend, categories, panels and time range as applicable. For percentages, establish what each share is a percentage of. For process diagrams, trace arrows, branch conditions and loops before drafting.
- Use labeled values or supplied data for exact numbers. Qualify estimates with *about*, *just under* or *just over* where the scale supports them. If a label or comparison is unreadable, describe the readable features and flag the missing information.
- Anchor each comparison to the correct group, denominator and period. Check calculated changes against the baseline. Describe visible patterns and attribute forecasts to the figure. Claims about causes or statistical significance require separate support. For research deliverables in this repo, follow AGENTS.md and record substantive claims with C-IDs, E-IDs and figure or data locators.

## Structure the description

For a full coursework response, follow the chapter's default structure. Adapt the length and format to the assignment. For a caption or manuscript paragraph, use the same selection principles within the requested format.

1. **Introduction.** In 1–2 sentences, paraphrase what the figure shows, including the measure, place or population, and period when provided. Preserve the original meaning of the metric.
2. **Overview.** In 2–3 sentences, summarize the dominant patterns, main contrasts or extremes. If the values fluctuate without a clear direction, describe that pattern and identify the relevant highs and lows. Start with *Overall* when it helps the reader. The overview may share the introductory paragraph. For a process or flowchart, it may also come at the end.
3. **Details.** Group related observations into short paragraphs, typically two or three. Support the overview with selected values, dates or stages. Choose figures that establish the comparison and leave routine values to the visual. Use a neutral register and deliver a clean English description by default.

## Choose what to describe

| Type | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Bar chart | Compare categories and series directly. Select the highest and lowest values, meaningful gaps and similarities. Group related categories or compare the same category across periods. Use constructions such as *higher than*, *nearly as high as*, *whereas* and *twice as high as*. |
| Line graph | Describe overall direction, turning points, peaks, troughs, plateaus and crossings when they matter. Group series with similar trends or divide the description by time period. Separate observed data from forecasts. Use the past simple for completed periods, the present perfect for a past trend that continues to the present, and *is projected to* or *is expected to* for an indicated forecast. Vary *rose steadily* with *there was a steady rise in* and sequence changes with *followed by* or *after rising*. |
| Pie chart | Identify the largest and smallest shares and compare matching slices in multiple charts. Use *accounted for*, *made up*, *the proportion of*, and fractions when they accurately convey the share. Use *the number of* for counts, *the amount of* for uncountable quantities, and *percent* after a numeral. |
| Table | Compare relevant rows or columns. Group similar or contrasting changes, or summarize ranks and exceptions when there is no time axis. Verify that row and column labels are paired correctly. |
| Process | Identify the beginning, inputs, main stages and outputs or return to the start. Group consecutive stages and use sequencing language such as *initially*, *once*, *then* and *finally*. Choose active or passive voice according to whether the actor matters. |
| Flowchart | Follow the direction of the arrows. Summarize the shared beginning and outcome, then describe each important branch with its condition. Mention merges or repeated steps only when the arrows show them. |

## Check numbers and wording

- Use *from A to B* for endpoints, *by X* for the size of a change, *at X* for a level, and *an increase of X in Y* for a nominal construction. Choose modifiers such as *slight*, *steady* or *sharp* to match the data and time scale.
- Illustrative arithmetic: a share changing from 20% to 25% *rose to 25%*, *rose by 5 percentage points*, or *rose by 25% relative to its initial value*. Use the formulation that suits the task.
- Before returning the text, check values, dates, comparison direction, units, tense and forecast wording against the visual. When revising a supplied draft, keep its intended meaning and flag substantive discrepancies with the figure.
